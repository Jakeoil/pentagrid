Discrete directions

A second geometry, and what happens if you dualize it

Everything else on this site is de Bruijn's: n families of parallel lines at exactly 2π/n, and a dual tiling in the corresponding cyclotomic field. This corner is about a different object. penrose-mosaic builds Penrose tilings on the integer lattice: every vertex has exact integer coordinates at every generation, and inflation is an integer substitution. The natural assumption is that such a construction is a rational approximant — that as it inflates, its directions converge to the Euclidean 36° and 72°. They do not. The substitution has its own dominant eigenvector, and its limiting slopes are algebraic of degree 2 over ℚ, in ℚ(√5) — a simpler field than the true tangents, which need nested radicals.

So there are two geometries, not one rounded off. This subproject is independent of the pentagrid pages: it shares the mathematics and the stylesheet, nothing else. The discrete arithmetic is adapted from penrose-mosaic's wheels — the substitution, its inverse, and the limit — and is checked here against that project's own stated results.

E2 — the wheel, and the directions a discrete multigrid would use

The first question, and the one everything else waits on: what are the discrete directions, exactly? The wheel at every generation and at the limit, the exact tangents, and the frame operator — which is where the trouble starts, because it is not isotropic.

Wiggle room

How much can γ move before the pattern changes?

The five offsets look like five degrees of freedom, and γ0 + ··· + γ4 = 0 cuts that to four. But two of those four do nothing at all. Shifting every γj by w · vj moves each crossing to x0 − w, and its K-tuple to

⌈ vi · (x0 − w) + γi + w · vi ⌉  =  ⌈ vi · x0 + γi ⌉

— unchanged. Same tuple, same vertex. The pentagrid slides and the tiling does not move. So the pattern depends on just two numbers: γ's component in the perpendicular plane, spanned by the same five angles doubled. That plane is the panel on the right.

Fix a patch of the tiling and ask which γ keep it. Each vertex survives exactly when its five strips still share a point — a linear condition — so the answer is a convex region, and every vertex you add to the patch can only shave it down. That region is the wiggle room, and you can measure it.

Inside the region the marked patch is intact. Cross the boundary and a vertex fails — hollow circles are the ones that no longer exist. That crossing is a phason flip: the tiling rearranges locally while everything far away stays put. Growing the patch shrinks the room, but not smoothly — most vertices you add were already forced by the ones inside them, and the area only drops when a genuinely new constraint binds.

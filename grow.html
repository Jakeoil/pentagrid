Growing the tiling

The pentagrid does not vanish — it gets dragged into shape

At t = 0 every tile is a point, sitting on the crossing that produced it, coloured by mixing the two families whose lines met there. So what you are looking at is the pentagrid, with a dot on every intersection.

Push t up and the tiles grow. They cannot all grow in place — they are crowded in with their neighbours — so each slides to where the dual map sends it. The grid lines are never redrawn: each stays fastened to the midpoints of the two tile edges it passes between, so it is pulled along, bending as the tiles separate. At t = 1 the tiles are full size and edge to edge, every line has closed up seamlessly, and the five straight families have become the wandering ribbons of the tiling.

Nothing is faked in the middle. The attachment points start exactly on the straight grid line, and each tile's exit meets the next tile's entry exactly when t reaches 1 — the gap between them closes in a straight line at a constant rate. The wandering you end up with is not drift; it is the bounded wobble in f (x) = (5/2)x + const + wobble, and it never exceeds a rhomb edge or so.

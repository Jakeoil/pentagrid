Moiré

Two deflation levels, overlaid — and what deflation does off the Penrose class

Nothing is built here yet. This page is a placeholder so the link in the bar above goes somewhere; the plan for it is PLAN.md § 12.

A generalized Penrose tiling is labeled by its LI class, Σγ mod 1, with Penrose at 0. Deflation sends γj to −(γj+2 + γj+3), and every row of that matrix sums to −2, so on the class it is the doubling map with a sign:

s → −2s (mod 1)

So a tiling off the Penrose class does not fail to deflate — it deflates into a tiling of a different class. What it stops being is self-similar. 0, 1/3 and 2/3 are the only classes that deflate to themselves; 1/2 lands in Penrose in a single step, 1/4 in two, 1/8 in three; 1/5 runs a four-cycle through 3/5, 4/5 and 2/5.

Which is why the two levels make a moiré. Measured on a patch and its deflation, each parent vertex is either exactly on a child vertex or exactly φ−2 away — never anything between. On Penrose it is always 0: every vertex of the parent is a vertex of the child, which is what the substitution says. Off it, the share that coincides is a property of the class rather than of the offsets — 81% at 1/8, 69% at 1/4, and a minimum of 59.6% at 1/2, symmetric about there. The moiré is that field of displacements.

Which is worth a second look: 1/2 resembles its own deflation least, and it is the class that deflates into Penrose in one step. Landing in Penrose and looking like your parent are different questions.
